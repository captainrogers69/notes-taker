Overall

The instinct is right: Weave moves little and moves fast. There are no pulses, no staggers, no entrances on static content, and reduced motion is honoured everywhere. What lets it down is the motion that is missing: the phone sheets and every menu snap in and out, and the ticked row vanishes and jolts the list. The one large movement, the bar flying into the tray, animates layout properties. Close the gaps, make exits lighter than enters, and put every state change on one strong curve.

Where the timings land

  8. 8No motion: phone sheets, phone menu, list switcher, desktop menusnone
Instant / Responsive · 0–300ms: where almost everything belongs. Taps, toggles, menus, ticks.
Deliberate · 300–500ms: earned by large surfaces and rare moves, like hiding the bar.
Sluggish · 500ms+: feels laggy. Reserve for rare, cinematic moments, or nothing.
What's off

The two most frequent moments sit furthest right: the tick holds for 280ms (4), and every new task glows for 1.2s (7). Meanwhile the ring at zero holds four surfaces that were meant to move and don't. The fly-to-tray (6) is rare, so its duration is fine, but it moves layout properties.

CriticalEmil

The bar flies into the tray by animating left and top

WhatHiding the bar transitions left, top, opacity and transform over 400ms, then waits on a matching setTimeout(400) before the tray icon pops. Showing the bar reverses it with a double requestAnimationFrame.

Why it mattersleft/top force layout on every frame. On a bar that sits over other apps, a dropped frame is the first sign of a heavy app. The fixed timeout also means a second click mid-flight can't retarget the animation.

Recommended motionLeave the bar's position where it is and animate only transform: translate(dx, dy) scale(.4) plus opacity toward the tray icon, at 260ms with an ease-out-quint curve. End on transitionend rather than a timer. The tray icon acknowledges with a small 1.12 bump, not 1.35.

ImportantEmilJakub

The tick holds for 280ms, then the row vanishes and the list jolts

WhatTicking fills the circle (150ms) and then waits 280ms on a timer. After that the whole list re-renders, so the row disappears in one frame and every row below jumps up. Tick two rows quickly and the first re-render wipes the second row's half-ticked state.

Why it mattersTicking is the action people repeat most. The hold is long enough to feel like waiting, and the jump moves the next target out from under the cursor or thumb. The beat itself is useful, because it confirms which row was ticked, but it should be shorter.

Recommended motionFill at 120ms. Fade the row out over 140ms (opacity only), then remove it and let the rows below close up over 160ms with a transform (FLIP), not a height animation. Keep each row's ticking state out of the re-render so rapid ticks don't cancel each other. Undo stays in the toast.

ImportantEmil

State changes run on the browser's default ease

WhatOnly some transforms use the token --ease. The sheet's opacity uses ease, and the check fill, Done chevron, toggle track, toast fade and phone scrim leave the timing function unset, which also means ease. Durations are spread across .08, .15, .16, .2, .26, .35 and .4s, with no shared tokens.

Why it mattersBuilt-in ease starts slow, so short transitions feel soft rather than crisp. Mixed curves on one surface (the sheet fades on one curve and scales on another) read as slightly unsteady even when nobody can say why.

Recommended motionAdd two duration tokens, --dur-fast: 120ms and --dur: 180ms, and one curve, --ease-out: cubic-bezier(.23,1,.32,1), in tokens.css. Use them on every transition. Give large surfaces (the phone sheet) their own curve, below.

Issues to address
CriticalJakub

Phone bottom sheets and the side menu snap in and out

WhatThe CSS describes a 260ms slide for the task, share and more sheets, the scrim fade and the menu. But render() rebuilds the whole phone screen with innerHTML, so the .on class lands on a brand-new element that has no "before" state to animate from. Every open and close happens in one frame.

Why it mattersOn a phone, a sheet that slides up tells you it's temporary and can be swiped away. One that teleports in reads as a new page and feels broken. This is the main way to edit a task on the phone.

Recommended motionKeep the scrim, sheet and menu as persistent nodes outside the re-rendered area, fill in their content, then toggle the class. Enter with translateY(100%) → 0 over 280ms on the iOS sheet curve cubic-bezier(.32,.72,0,1). Exit faster, at 200ms ease-in, and fade the scrim alongside. The menu slides in from the left on the same values.

ImportantJakubEmil

Menus pop with no enter or exit: the list switcher and Share, More and Colour

WhatIn the sheet, the list switcher toggles display. On desktop, the Share, More and Colour menus toggle hidden. Each appears and disappears in one frame, with no sense of where it came from.

Why it mattersA menu that grows from its trigger tells you what opened it and where to look. Snapping is the "half-built" tell, and the switcher is opened several times a day.

Recommended motionOn a click, use opacity plus translateY(-4px) scale(.97) → none over 150ms ease-out, with transform-origin at the trigger: top-left for the list switcher and Colour, top-right for Share and More. Exit with a 100ms opacity fade. When Backspace opens the switcher, keep it instant, because that's keyboard input.

ImportantJakub

Exits are as heavy as enters: the sheet and the toast

WhatClosing the sheet plays its enter in reverse (scale .96, 6px slide, 200ms). The toast leaves with the same 16px drop and 200ms it arrived with.

Why it mattersOn close, the user has already moved on. A full reverse makes them watch it leave, and the sheet is closed many times a day. Lighter exits make the whole app feel faster without touching the enters.

Recommended motionKeep the enters. Exit with opacity over 120ms plus scale(.99) and no slide. Fade the toast out over 150ms without the drop.

Which lens carried this audit

Emil Kowalski — Restraint & Speed

Weave is a tool on screen all day, so every recommendation was first asked whether it should move at all, and how often it fires. Jakub's lens found the craft gaps (missing enters and exits, heavy exits), and Jhey's marked the only two places worth spending delight.

  • Lean EmilDrop the new-task highlight entirely, make the tick fill-only with no row exit, and keep menus at 100ms or instant.
  • Lean JakubAdd blur(2px) to the sheet enter, FLIP every list reorder (adding, ticking, moving up/down), and animate the Done expand.
  • Lean JheyBuild the end-of-day thread moment with @property-driven ink and a first-run unroll, and keep both one-shot.
